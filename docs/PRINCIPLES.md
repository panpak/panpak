# Panpak guiding principles

1. **The user is our lighthouse.** We start from what a person wants to do: find an app, know whom to trust, open a file, get back to work. We judge every choice by how much it improves that experience.
2. **Faster.** We cut the wait from the click to the first editable document, from the command to the result, and from the tag to a ready package. We also measure the bytes downloaded and the disk space used over the whole life of an app.
3. **Safer.** Every package has a recognisable publisher and a verifiable origin. Permissions match what the user meant: choosing a photo grants access to that photo. New permissions and changes of origin require a conscious choice.
4. **Simpler, for those who use it and those who build it.** Users find the app where they already look for it. Developers ship it from their own build process. Panpak computes what it can, shows what changes between two releases and says exactly what is missing when a test fails.
5. **Universal, in practice.** The same signed package can come from the author's website, a community catalog or a distribution's store. Every distribution can verify the build it offers. We prove compatibility by actually using the apps on the systems we say we support.
6. **Learn from the other formats, and work with them.** Flatpak, Snap and AppImage solved hard problems, and we learn a lot from them. We compare Panpak like for like, in realistic conditions and with reproducible tests, to give users the best experience we can. We publish where others do better, too. For every gap that matters to users, we find the cause, try a fix and measure again. We credit what we take from others and, where we can, give back: shared standards such as portals, bug reports and fixes upstream.
7. **Less is more.** Every recipe field, dependency, option and confirmation prompt costs someone something. We keep only what is needed. When we solve a problem for one app, we look for a way to spare it to those who come after. We build the foundations first, and on top of them only what holds.
8. **Never settle.** Even when a solution works, we ask whether there is a better one, and we write down what we discarded and why. What we do well today is the starting point for doing it better tomorrow. An improvement is proven with a test and with numbers, not with another pass on paper.
